# Process Vision -- Conversation Notes

## Core Idea

This conversation started as a question about whether there were books
that explain the *logic* of sewing rather than simply giving
step-by-step instructions.

Very quickly it became clear that the real topic wasn't sewing.

It was **how some people naturally see systems instead of objects.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# The Engineering of Sewing

## Premise

Most sewing books teach *what* to do.

This book would teach *why*.

Instead of:

> Sew A to B.

It explains:

-   Why that seam has to happen first.
-   Why everything is sewn inside out.
-   Why seam allowances disappear.
-   Why construction order matters.
-   How flat fabric becomes a 3D object.

The goal is to think like a pattern maker instead of memorizing
patterns.

Potential chapter ideas:

-   The Great Inside-Out Mystery
-   Flat Things Become Round
-   The Math Nobody Explains
-   Fabric Has Physics
-   Order Matters
-   Sewing the Invisible
-   Quilt Logic

# Bigger Idea: Process Vision

## Working Title

**Process Vision** *Why Some People See Systems Instead of Objects*

Opening idea:

> Every object you've ever touched is the final frame of a movie.

Most people see the final frame.

Some people instinctively rewind the movie.

They see:

-   Invisible factories
-   Invisible decisions
-   Invisible constraints
-   Invisible tradeoffs
-   Invisible people
-   Invisible systems

## My Way of Thinking

A realization during the conversation:

I don't primarily see objects.

I see the process that created them.

Examples:

### Bagel

Most people:

> One bagel is smaller.

My brain:

-   How are bagels divided?
-   Where did the manufacturing process vary?
-   Was the divider off?
-   Did it proof differently?
-   Did it bake differently?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Costco

Most people:

> That's a giant bag of rice.

My brain:

-   Global supply chains
-   Purchasing power
-   Warehouse logistics
-   Packaging
-   Shipping
-   Economies of scale
-   Why Costco can sell it so cheaply
-   Why a small grocery store charges more

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Craft Supplies

Buying a small amount of yarn or fabric makes me think about:

-   Manufacturers buying pallets
-   Wholesale pricing
-   Industrial scale
-   Consumer markups
-   Why I pay more for tiny quantities

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Restaurants

Food isn't just food.

I think about:

-   Where ingredients came from
-   Who touched them
-   Kitchen workflow
-   Refrigeration
-   Transportation
-   Waste

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Sewing

A bag isn't a bag.

It's a sequence of engineering decisions.

Questions become:

-   Why this seam first?
-   Why leave this opening?
-   Why turn here?
-   What constraint required this order?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Jimothy

One of the most revealing moments.

Someone wanted to buy my crocheted raccoon ("Jimothy").

My response:

> I'm not going to make another. My crochet projects are one and done.

That led to an insight.

I don't think I create because I want more finished objects.

I create because I want to solve the puzzle.

Once I understand the system...

...the mystery is gone.

Making a second identical object feels like manufacturing, not
discovery.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Exploration vs Repetition

The conversation suggested that my interests follow a pattern.

1.  Find a fascinating system.
2.  Learn how it works.
3.  Build something.
4.  Prove I understand it.
5.  Move on to another system.

This explains why my interests change while the underlying process never
does.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Cognitive Themes

Words that describe this way of thinking:

-   Systems thinking
-   Reverse engineering
-   Causal reasoning
-   First-principles thinking
-   Mental models
-   Model-based thinking
-   Integrative thinking

Important note:

These traits can overlap with neurodivergence, but they are not by
themselves evidence of autism or ADHD. They are also common among
engineers, designers, inventors, scientists, and entrepreneurs.
